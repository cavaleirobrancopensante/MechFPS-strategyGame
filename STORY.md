# Hive Breakers — Story Bible

## The invasion
Eleven weeks ago a ringed world appeared beside the Moon. Nobody saw it
arrive; it was simply there one evening, huge and amber, and everyone
started calling it **the Lantern**. Three nights later it rained seeds.

Where a seed lands, a **hive** grows: a pulsing mound of alien flesh that
sings at a pitch you feel in your teeth. The singing calls **the Swarm**:
crawlers that run in packs, spitters that lob acid, and brutes the size of
a lorry. Hives spread, the Swarm spreads with them, and the song gets
louder. The crews call it **the Choir**.

Cities fell first. What is left of the world holds out in pockets.

## The Rock
**St. Columba's Rock** is a small island off the western coast: a
cliff, a lighthouse, and a monastery that has stood for nine hundred
years. A coastguard unit retreated there in the first week because the
Swarm hates crossing open water. Under the monastery they found old
smugglers' caves deep enough to hide a hangar.

Now the Rock is home to a few dozen people, one dropship and one combat
walker. The abbey kitchen still makes bread every morning. The bees in
the cloister garden are still alive. When the dropship comes home, the
bell in the tower is rung once for every crew member aboard. Nobody has
ever missed the count.

Places on the Rock: the cloister garden and beehives, the bell tower, the
refectory (kitchen and mess), the chapel (now an infirmary), the
library (now the map room), the old quarry, the boathouse, and the
hangar caves under the cliff.

## The people
- **Wren** — you. The only pilot left who is qualified on the walker.
  Quiet, stubborn, the one they drop into the worst places.
- **Commander Nora Quill** — ex-coastguard, runs the Rock. Calm on the
  radio, dry as old toast in person. Writes every name on the map-room wall.
- **Brother Anselm** — the last monk, who refused to leave. Keeps the bees,
  bakes the bread, rings the bell. Gentle, funny, and harder than he looks.
- **Mags Okonkwo** — chief engineer. Runs the workshop and the ammo
  factory. Talks to the walker more than to people. Named it **Brigid**.
- **Dr. Ivo Sandoval** — xenobiologist. Thinks the Swarm is the most
  beautiful thing he has ever seen and wants to kill every bit of it.
  Turns alien biomass into fuel and research.
- **Rafa "Gannet" Ibarra** — dropship pilot. His ship is the *Gannet*.
  Drops you into hot zones with a joke and always, always comes back.
- **Pip Doyle** — radio operator and drone scout, nineteen, talks fast,
  hears the Choir in the static and tracks it.

## The walker
**Brigid** is a Mk II Bastion walker, twice the height of a person, with
twin autocannons and a shoulder rocket pod. Too wide for alleys and
doorways, which is why the pilot carries a carbine and sometimes has to
climb out and walk.

## How a mission works
1. Pick an operation in the map room. Each costs dropship fuel.
2. The *Gannet* drops Brigid into the hot zone.
3. Do the job: destroy hives, rescue survivors, recover cargo, light up
   relay beacons, or hold out against wave after wave.
4. Reach the landing zone and hold it until Rafa can set down.
5. Night at the Rock: the base produces, the crew talks, the bell rings.

## Why not just level everything with rockets?
Brigid's rockets can knock ruins and walls down to rubble, and sometimes
that is exactly the right answer: blow a hole through a building instead
of walking around it. But:
- **The places worth going into are bunkers.** Pre-war blast shelters,
  built to survive bombs. Rockets don't scratch them. That is why people
  hid there, and why the doors are narrow.
- **Explosions kill what's inside.** A rocket near survivors kills them.
  Near cargo, it destroys the cargo. Near a relay beacon, it resets it.
- **Rockets are precious.** Each one costs scrap and circuits from the
  ammo factory, and Brigid only carries a few.

So you level what's in the way, and you walk in for what matters.

## The economy of the end of the world
Money is worthless now. What matters:
- **Scrap** — metal from wrecks, caches and the quarry crane. Builds and repairs everything.
- **Circuits** — electronics, rare. Rockets, research and radio gear.
- **Biomass** — what is left of dead aliens. Sandoval's bioreactor turns it into fuel and research.
- **Fuel** — every flight of the *Gannet* burns it.
- **Ammunition** — cannon shells, rockets and carbine rounds, made in Mags' ammo factory from scrap.
- **Survivors** — everyone rescued becomes part of the crew and staffs the base.

## The story arc
**Act I — Landfall.** Wren's first drops: burning the first hive near the
coast, pulling survivors out of a flooded town, raiding a fuel depot, and
a desperate stand on a ridge when the *Gannet* loses an engine.

**Act II — The Singing.** Pip discovers the hives are talking to each
other. Wren lights up old radio relays so the Rock can listen. Sandoval
needs a living hive core. The Choir answers with bigger swarms, and more
people need saving.

**Act III — The Brood Mother.** The song has a source: a vast mother-hive
feeding every nest in the region. A crashed airliner's black box gives
its location. After one long night holding the line, Wren drops into the
Brood Mother's nest to silence it.

**After the story:** the region is quieter, but the Lantern is still in
the sky. The war goes on in endless operations.
